CelesteCon 2026 // Regulations & Conduct PAGE 03 // GENERAL DIRECTIVES
General Guidelines & Code of Conduct SEC // 02
01. REPORTING & DESK ACCREDITATION

All registered participating schools and independent delegates must report at the DPS R.K. Puram Welcome Foyer strictly between 07:30 AM and 08:30 AM on the day of the onsite event. Late arrivals will forfeit participation in preliminary heats. Delegate identity credentials will be issued upon arrival.

02. UNIFORM & INSTITUTIONAL ATTIRE

All students must be dressed in their official school uniform (or designated institutional team blazer). For AEROSS Prix pit crews and AEROSS Theatre stage performers, custom team livery or costume changes are permissible exclusively during their designated live performance windows.

03. HARDWARE & WORKSTATION SAFETY

Participants must carry their own laptops, chargers, adapters, 3D CAD files, and specialized hardware accessories. The host school will provide standard power outlets and campus Wi-Fi, but delegates are strongly advised to ensure laptops and devices arrive with full battery backup. The host school accepts no liability for personal hardware.

04. SCHEDULE OVERLAPS & CROSS-PARTICIPATION

A student is permitted to enter multiple competitions across the convention. However, timings for on-campus parallel rounds, stage appearances, and defense schedules may overlap. It remains the sole responsibility of the student and teacher coordinator to manage attendance across clashing schedules.

05. OVERALL ROLLING TROPHY ACCREDITATION CRITERIA

The coveted CelesteCon 2026 Overall Champions Rolling Trophy will be awarded to the institution that demonstrates supreme competitive excellence across all disciplines.

  • Point Structure: 1st Place = 100 pts | 2nd Place = 70 pts | 3rd Place = 40 pts | Finalist Qualifying = 15 pts.
  • Compulsory Breadth: To be eligible for the Overall Rolling Trophy, a school contingent must field entries in a minimum of 6 out of the 8 competitions.
  • Tie-Breaker: In the event of a point tie, highest number of 1st-place victories serves as the primary tiebreaker, followed by performance in engineering categories (Settle-Me-This and Volatus).
06. HOST SCHOOL STATUS

As per long-standing tradition of Delhi Public School, R.K. Puram, teams representing the host school participate on a strictly non-competitive basis. They will be evaluated by juries for benchmarking, but are ineligible for ranking positions, podium trophies, or the Overall Champions trophy.

07. JURY SOVEREIGNTY & ARBITRATION

In all competitions, the scoring, rubric assessments, and ranking verdicts of the Grand Juries, external industry adjudicators, and faculty observers are final, non-negotiable, and binding. Disrespect towards arbiters or marshals results in immediate disqualification.

Event Dossier // Flagship 01 PAGE 05 // SETTLE-ME-THIS
Settle-Me-This (Space Settlement Design) EVENT 01
Hybrid Mode Grades 6–8 (Jr) & 9–12 (Sr) Team of 3 to 5 Members Max 3 Teams per School

Space colonisation has advanced far beyond lunar outposts or viewing Mars as the only alternate habitat. With rapid advancements in materials, life support, and orbital dynamics, permanent free-space settlements situated at Lagrange points represent humanity's next planetary leap. Settle-Me-This invites teams to author an engineering proposal for a fully functioning, self-sustaining orbital settlement.

Round 1: Comprehensive Engineering Proposal (Online Submission) STAGE 1

Teams author and submit an exhaustive technical proposal addressing four core engineering domains:

  • Structural & Rotational Dynamics: Station geometry (torus, cylinder, dumbbell), pressure vessel materials, rotational rate and radius sized for 1G artificial gravity, and docking hubs.
  • Life Support & Environmental Control (ECLSS): Closed-loop water/air regeneration, agriculture, cosmic radiation shielding (regolith/water jackets), and thermal management.
  • Logistics, Communications & Power Architecture: Solar concentrators/nuclear reactors, station attitude control, telemetry arrays, orbital debris protection, and industrial manufacturing bays.
  • Community Infrastructure & Emergency Decompression: Demographic distribution, commercial economy, zoning, and multi-compartment bulkhead quarantine protocols.

Max Page Limits: 25 Pages (Junior Division) / 35 Pages (Senior Division). PDF Format.

Round 2: On-Campus Presentation & Injection Defense (Onsite Finals) STAGE 2
  • 10-Minute Technical Defense: Qualifying finalist teams present their settlement architecture to an aerospace panel using slides, digital CAD models, or physical mockups.
  • 05-Minute Jury Cross-Examination: Technical grilling into rotational stress calculations, radiation shielding thickness, mass budgets, and ecological sustainability.
  • 03-Minute Injection Challenge: Teams receive an unexpected, spontaneous operational crisis scenario (e.g. rapid micrometeoroid puncture in agriculture module or solar flare power blackout) and must deliver an immediate contingency defense under pressure.

Event Dossier // Flagship 02 PAGE 06 // VOLATUS
Volatus (Aviation / UAV & Dimension III) EVENT 02
Hybrid Mode Grades 6–8 (Jr) & 9–12 (Sr) Team of 2 to 4 Members Max 3 Teams per School

Merging flight dynamics, aerodynamics, and digital 3D prototyping (incorporating the legacy of Dimension III), Volatus challenges students to solve real-world aviation challenges. Teams design an uncrewed aerial vehicle (UAV/eVTOL) to meet stringent mission profiles, complete with aerodynamic calculations, structural packaging in CAD, and an oral flight review.

Round 1: Engineering Dossier & 3D CAD Assembly (Online Submission) STAGE 1
  • Mission Profile & Aerodynamic Sizing: Aircraft mission parameters, wing geometry (aspect ratio, sweep, dihedral), NACA airfoil selection, drag polar calculations, and thrust-to-weight estimation.
  • Weight, Balance & Stability: Mass breakdown, Center of Gravity (CG) margin calculations, static longitudinal stability, and control surface sizing (ailerons, elevators, rudders).
  • Complete 3D CAD Assembly Submission: Submission of native 3D CAD files (.STEP, .F3D, or .BLEND) including exploded assembly diagrams, isometric renderings, internal avionics bay packaging, and structural rib spar drawings.

Permitted Software: Autodesk Fusion 360, SolidWorks, Blender, Onshape, AutoCAD, OpenVSP.

Round 2: Onsite Flight Review & Exhibition (Onsite Finals) STAGE 2
  • Interactive CAD Walkthrough: Finalists display their designs at an exhibition station. Teams provide real-time CAD model walkthroughs, cross-sectional views, and optional physical scale prototypes.
  • Jury Flight Review (10 Minutes): Defense of stability derivatives, payload packaging, motor selection, and structural stress tolerances before aviation adjudicators.
  • Curveball Engineering Challenge: Teams receive an unexpected operational constraint (e.g. payload mass increased by 30% or emergency gust alleviation requirement) and must justify aerodynamic modifications.

Event Dossier // Flagship 03 PAGE 07 // IN PURSUIT OF DISPUTE
In Pursuit of Dispute (Debate & Quizzitch) EVENT 03
Hybrid Mode Grades 6–12 (Open Division) Team of 2 to 3 Members Max 2 Teams per School

The premier aerospace forensics championship of CelesteCon 2026, integrating Quizzitch preliminaries with a high-stakes parliamentary debate tournament. This competition tests deep aerospace technical literacy, geopolitical and legal acumen, and spontaneous rhetorical clash under intense chess-clock pressure.

Round 1: Quizzitch Screening & Case Video (Online Prelims) STAGE 1
  • Quizzitch Prelims: An intensive online proctored aerospace quiz examining astrophysics trivia, orbital mechanics, planetary science, and the history of spaceflight.
  • Case Video Presentation (3 Minutes): Teams submit a concise 3-minute video defending a stance on an assigned preliminary motion regarding orbital debris liability, lunar commercial mining, or space militarization.
  • Top ranked qualifying teams from the combined score advance to the Onsite Chess-Clock Finals.
Round 2: Onsite Chess-Clock Finals (On-Campus Clashes) STAGE 2
  • Live Parliamentary Tournament: Bracketed head-to-head debate rounds conducted live at DPS R.K. Puram. Motions released 48 hours prior to the onsite event.
  • Chess-Clock Time Management: Each side receives a 15-minute shared digital time bank. Teams allocate their time dynamically between constructive speeches, cross-examinations, and rebuttals.
  • Points of Information (POIs): Opponents may introduce POIs during open speech windows. Teams are scored heavily on clash engagement, factual evidence, and rhetorical composure under ticking clock limits.

Event Dossier // Flagship 04 PAGE 08 // BUSINESS POWER PITCH
Business Power Pitch (Aerospace Venture) EVENT 04
Hybrid Mode Grades 6–8 (Jr) & 9–12 (Sr) Team of 2 to 4 Members Max 3 Teams per School

True aerospace innovation demands visionary engineering paired with viable unit economics and compelling market strategy. Business Power Pitch challenges teams to conceptualize a commercial aerospace venture—spanning space resource utilization, satellite constellations, launch logistics, space tourism, or sustainable aviation—and defend it before venture capitalists.

Round 1: Executive Memo & Pitch Video (Online Submission) STAGE 1
  • Executive Business Memo: Comprehensive proposal covering problem validation, technical novelty, Total Addressable Market (TAM/SAM/SOM), revenue monetization model, competitor differentiation, and 5-year financial projections.
  • 5-Minute Pitch Deck Video: Recorded investor pitch deck walking through the business proposition, team capabilities, capital requirements, and risk mitigation strategies.
Round 2: Live Investor Shark Tank (Onsite Finals) STAGE 2
  • 08-Minute Shark Tank Presentation: Finalist teams pitch live on stage in the Main Auditorium before an esteemed panel of venture capitalists, angel investors, and industry founders.
  • 06-Minute Investor Cross-Examination: Deep probing into customer acquisition costs (CAC), lifetime value (LTV), regulatory hurdles (FAA/ITU/IN-SPACe), launch contracts, and scalability.
  • Audience Challenger Questions: Competing teams in the audience may submit challenger questions for bonus points.

Event Dossier // Flagship 05 PAGE 09 // CELESTEJAM
CelesteJam (Game Development & Simulation) EVENT 05
Onsite Mode Grades 6–12 (Open Division) Team of 1 to 3 Members Max 3 Teams per School

CelesteJam is CelesteCon's premier game engineering challenge. Teams are tasked with designing, programming, and polishing an original, fully playable aerospace minigame or physics simulation to be showcased live on campus for peer playtesting, arcade evaluation, and jury inspection.

Phase 1: 72-Hour Theme Sprint & GDD Submission PHASE 1
  • Surprise Theme Release: The core mechanic constraints and theme are revealed 72 hours prior to submission on the official CelesteCon Discord server.
  • Playable Prototype & GDD: Teams submit a standalone executable or WebGL link alongside a 2-page Game Design Document (GDD) detailing core loop, input mapping, physics mechanics, and technical architecture.
  • Permitted Engines: Unity, Unreal Engine, Godot, WebGL / Three.js, Python / Pygame, Raylib / C++. All game code must be original; open-source third-party assets must be fully cited.
Phase 2: Arcade Expo, Playtesting & Patch Sprint (Onsite Finals) PHASE 2
  • Arcade Expo Booths: Finalists set up playable kiosks in the computer lab arena. Delegates and jury members playtest each game and submit peer performance ratings.
  • 45-Minute Surprise Patch Sprint: During the live event, teams receive an on-the-spot "Curveball Patch Directive" (e.g. integrate inverted gravity mechanic or new hazard obstacle) and must push an updated build live within 45 minutes!

Event Dossier // Flagship 07 PAGE 11 // ROCKETRY
Rocketry (Craft, Scrutineering & Simulation) EVENT 07
Onsite Mode Grades 6–8 (Jr) & 9–12 (Sr) Individual or Team of 1 to 3 Members Max 2 Teams per School

Rocketry is CelesteCon's hands-on aerospace fabrication and aerodynamic simulation challenge. Participants construct high-precision scale rockets or flight-capable vehicle models. Rather than live field launches, rockets undergo rigorous static inspection, dimensional scrutiny, and computational flight simulation testing to evaluate whether the craft is aerodynamically sound, properly balanced, and genuinely capable of flight.

Single Onsite Round: Scrutineering & Simulation (Campus Competition) COMPREHENSIVE SESSION
  • Physical Model Scrutineering: Teams bring their fabricated rocket craft to campus. Adjudicators inspect fin alignment, root-chord bonding, symmetry, payload bay tolerances, and body tube surface finish.
  • Barrowman Stability & Balance Verification: Verification of Center of Gravity (CG) vs Center of Pressure (CP). Craft must demonstrate a positive static stability margin (1.0 to 2.0 calibers).
  • Computational Flight Simulation: Teams present verified digital flight simulations (OpenRocket / RockSim) modeling motor thrust-to-weight, apogee estimation, velocity curves, and parachute deployment dynamics.
  • Recovery Bay Mechanism Inspection: Hands-on inspection of parachute, streamer, or glide recovery deployment systems.
SAFETY DIRECTIVE: STRICT PROHIBITION OF LIVE MOTORS

Physical rockets will NOT be launched into the air at the venue. All flight capabilities are assessed via software simulation and physical bench testing. Under no circumstances may live pyrotechnic rocket motors, solid propellants, igniters, or chemical fuels be brought onto school grounds.

Event Dossier // Flagship 08 PAGE 12 // AEROSS PRIX
AEROSS Prix (Miniature F1 Motorsport) EVENT 08
Onsite Mode Grades 6–8 (Jr) & 9–12 (Sr) Team of 3 to 5 Members Max 3 Teams per School

AEROSS Prix is CelesteCon's premier miniature Formula 1 engineering and track racing challenge. Teams design, manufacture, brand, and race miniature CO2-cartridge powered racing cars along a 20-meter high-speed track. AEROSS Prix merges computational fluid dynamics (CFD), CNC/3D manufacturing, aerodynamic downforce analysis, enterprise pit branding, and lightning-fast track sprints.

Single Onsite Round: Scrutineering, Pit Defense & Track Races COMPREHENSIVE SESSION
  • Phase 1: Technical Scrutineering: Physical inspection of car dimensional envelope, minimum weight thresholds, wheel track clearance, tether line guides, and CO2 canister chamber alignment.
  • Phase 2: Enterprise & Pit Wall Defense: Oral presentation of CAD/CFD design, manufacturing process, aerodynamic drag reduction, and team livery at their custom pit display booth.
  • Phase 3: Official Track Races: Head-to-head sprint racing down the official 20-meter track powered by standard 8g CO2 canisters. Laser-sensor timing gates record reaction time and elapsed sprint times.
TRACK REGULATIONS & CANISTER SAFETY

Standard 8-gram CO2 canisters will be provided and loaded exclusively by official track marshals at the launch gate. All wheels must rotate freely and maintain continuous track contact. Cars must withstand rapid deceleration at the braking gate without chassis fragmentation.
