2.1Designing around the D12

Motor mount

A 24 mm motor tube at least 70 mm deep, with a motor hook or retainer to hold it. Judges check the fit with a dummy 24 × 70 mm casing.

In your .ork

Use the Estes D12-5 entry exactly. No custom or edited thrust curves, and no extra mass added for the motor: OpenRocket adds it.

Weight

Estes rates the D12-5 for up to 283 g at liftoff, motor included. Lighter rockets fly higher.

The 5 s delay

The parachute charge fires 5 s after burnout. OpenRocket shows an "optimum delay" for your design. The closer it is to 5 s, the nearer apogee your chute opens.

2.2What's allowed

Allowed

Not allowed

  • Undeclared commercial parts
  • Custom or edited motor files in the .ork

  • Any motor, propellant or igniter on site

Step 3 · Event day

What happens on 24 October

Bring the rocket to your display station. It goes through three stages, and you don't touch the simulation.

  1. Check-in & safety check

    Judges look inside to confirm there's no motor or anything else that's banned, and test the mount with a dummy casing.

  2. Rocket vs file

    Scrutineers measure the rocket and compare every number with your .ork. Mismatches cost points.

  3. Simulation scoring

    Your measured values go into OpenRocket under the same standard conditions for every team.

Step 4 · Judging

Three things decide it

Juniors and Seniors are ranked separately. In both, a high apogee alone won't carry a rocket that doesn't match its file or wasn't built with care.

CRITERION A

Simulated flight performance

  • Apogee
  • Stability margin between 1.0 and 2.5 calibers throughout the burn
  • Flight behaviour using your measured parameters

CRITERION B

Digital-to-physical fidelity

  • How closely the built rocket matches your .ork
  • Checked on the scale and with calipers
  • Differences cost points

CRITERION C

Build quality & craftsmanship

  • Fin alignment and fillets
  • Structural rigidity
  • Smooth surface finish
  • Recovery system packaging

The stability band. Margin is the gap between CP and CG, divided by body diameter. Below 1.0 the rocket can wobble or tumble. Above 2.5 it is overstable and weathercocks hard into any wind.

How this is calculated

CP uses the Barrowman equations for a nose cone plus one fin set, ignoring body lift and transitions. The nose contributes a normal-force coefficient of 2, centred at 0.466 × length for an ogive, 0.666 for a cone and 0.5 for a parabolic nose.

CG at liftoff combines your rocket's mass and CG with the loaded motor, assumed to sit with its aft end flush with the end of the body tube. At burnout the propellant mass is removed.

Margin = (CP − CG) ÷ body diameter. OpenRocket uses a fuller model, so expect its numbers to differ slightly. The judges' simulation is the one that counts.

The example is a 41.6 mm rocket, about 61 cm long, with a 24 mm motor mount. Replace it with your own numbers.

Step 6 · Rules & contacts

Rules & contacts

How to submit your file, how the day runs, and who to ask.

6.1Submitting your .ork

  1. Open the Submission Portal with your School UID.
  2. Upload your OpenRocket file with the Estes D12-5 selected and every pre-made part declared.
  3. Submit by 22 October. Check the portal for the exact cut-off time.
  4. Keep your Token of Submission. You need it to check in on 24 October.

6.2Conclave rules

Reporting

Welcome Foyer, DPS R.K. Puram, 07:45–08:45 AM. Late arrivals can lose their measurement slot. IDs are issued on arrival; bring your Token of Submission.

Display station

After check-in, set the rocket up at your display station. Judges come to you to measure it.

Host school

DPS R.K. Puram teams compete for benchmarking only. They can't take rankings, podium trophies or the Overall trophy.

Hardware

Bring your own laptops, chargers and adapters, fully charged. Power outlets are provided; the host accepts no liability for personal hardware. OpenRocket runs offline, so you don't need internet.

Judges' decisions

Judges' decisions are final. Disrespect towards judges or event staff means immediate disqualification.

Attire

School uniform is fine. Any outfit appropriate for a school setting is allowed.
