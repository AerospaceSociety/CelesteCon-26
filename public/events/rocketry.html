Rocketry
Ignite · Ascend · Recover

The Hands-on Model Rocketry, Aerospace Craftsmanship & Propulsion Challenge.

From Tsiolkovsky's founding rocket equation to multi-stage orbital launchers, true aerospace engineering begins with physical propulsion. Rocketry challenges student teams to engineer, balance, fabricate, and test-launch physical aerospace vehicles under strict aerodynamic and range-safety parameters.

In Round 1, teams submit a comprehensive Engineering Dossier documenting structural CAD design, motor impulse sizing, recovery parachute deployment mechanics, and Barrowman aerodynamic stability margin ($X_{cp} - X_{cg}$). In Round 2, qualifying teams assemble at the designated launch range on campus for structural scrutineering and an official test flight review before a jury of aerospace researchers.

01

Competition Tracks

Two distinct disciplines catering to scale master craftsmanship and high-performance aerodynamic flight testing.

Track A

Static Scale & Orbital Craft

Designed for scale fidelity, museum-grade craftsmanship, and historic aerospace replication. Open to both Junior (Grades 6–8) and Senior (Grades 9–12) divisions.

  • Subject Matter: Real-world orbital launch vehicles (e.g. PSLV, LVM3, Saturn V, Falcon 9, Ariane 6, SLS, Starship) or robotic deep-space probes.
  • Scale Fidelity: Strict adherence to true geometric aspect ratios, stage separation mechanisms, interstage trusses, and thermal protection tiling.
  • Materials: Balsa wood, composite resin, 3D printing (PLA/PETG), lightweight aluminum, styrene, and high-density foam. Non-flight model.
  • Deliverable: Completed display craft, scale calculation docket, and paint liveries accompanied by an engineering process logbook.
Track B

Working Propulsion & Flight Launch

High-stakes flight testing with powered ascent and safe parachute recovery on the campus launch pad. Open to Senior teams (Grades 9–12).

  • Propulsion Categories: Class 1 Commercial Model Rocket Solid Motors (Estes A, B, C, D impulse ratings) OR High-Pressure Hydro-Pneumatic Multi-Stage Water Rockets (≤ 100 PSI with certified pressure relief valves).
  • Aerodynamic Verification: Mandatory Barrowman stability calculation showing static margin between 1.0 and 2.5 calibers before launch clearance.
  • Recovery System: Deployed ejection charge streamer, mechanical spring baffle, or dual-deployment parachute with drift velocity ≤ 5 m/s.
  • Telemetry & Scrutineering: Electronic altimeter data logging (optional bonus) and mandatory pre-launch range officer inspection.
02

Aerodynamic Principles & Barrowman Math

True rocket design demands rigorous mathematics to guarantee flight path stability and prevent aerodynamic tumble.

The stability of an unguided model rocket in atmospheric flight is governed entirely by the relative positions of its Center of Gravity ($X_{cg}$) and its Center of Pressure ($X_{cp}$). As long as the CP lies behind the CG, any angle of attack ($\alpha$) induced by crosswinds produces a restoring aeromechanical torque that aligns the rocket back into the relative wind vector.

Subsystem Technical Design Standard Evaluation Parameter
NOSECONE Von Kármán, Parabolic, or Ogive profile to minimize subsonic wave drag and skin friction. Shape factor fineness ratio $L/D \ge 3.0$.
BODY TUBE Spiral-wound kraft phenolic tubing or thin-wall carbon composite; smooth seam finishing. Cylindrical uniformity; motor tube centering rings with engine block.
FIN CAN 3 or 4 symmetrical clipped delta or swept-back trapezoidal fins with airfoiled edges. True perpendicular alignment; root fillet adhesive strength > 25 N shear.
RECOVERY Fire-resistant recovery wadding; ripstop nylon or polyethylene parachute with braided shroud lines. Shock cord length $\ge 3\times$ body length; positive deployment ejection seal.

04

Range Safety Code & Launch Protocols

Safety is the foundational rule of rocketry. Zero exceptions are made to the range protocol.

Protocol ID Mandatory Range Rule Operational Penalty
R-01.PROPELLANT Only commercially manufactured, factory-sealed and certified model rocket motors (or pneumatic water assemblies) are permitted. Absolutely NO homemade propellants, match heads, potassium nitrate mixes, or explosive chemicals. Immediate disqualification and safety escort off campus.
R-02.IGNITION Launches must use an electrical launch system with a safety interlock key and spring-loaded launch button located at least 5 meters away from the launch pad for model rockets. Launch aborted until compliant controller connected.
R-03.RECOVERY Every flying vehicle must incorporate an engineered recovery system (parachute, streamer, or gliding wings) that returns it safely without damage to spectators or property. Disqualification from Track B flight scoring.
R-04.WIND LIMIT No rocket shall be launched in surface winds exceeding 30 km/h (8 m/s) or toward low-altitude cloud cover. Range Hold until atmospheric clearance.
05

Evaluation Matrix & Scoring Rubric

Scored out of 100 points by our panel of aerospace researchers and engineering alumni.

Technical Dossier (Round 1) — 40 Pts

  • Structural & CAD Modeling (15 pts): Quality of orthographic blueprints, dimensioned exploded views, and aerodynamic surface smoothness.
  • Barrowman Mathematical Validation (15 pts): Detailed calculations verifying stability margin under full and spent propellant states.
  • Recovery & Ejection Sizing (10 pts): Parachute drag sizing, descent rate calculation, and thermal protection design.

Onsite Flight Review (Round 2) — 60 Pts

  • Craftsmanship & Structural Scrutineering (20 pts): Fin alignment, body rigidity, launch lug clearance, and motor retention integrity.
  • Flight Performance & Stability (25 pts): Straight trajectory, minimal weathercocking, clean staging, and apogee deployment.
  • Technical Defense & Q&A (15 pts): Ability of all team members to defend engineering decisions and explain recovery aerodynamics.
